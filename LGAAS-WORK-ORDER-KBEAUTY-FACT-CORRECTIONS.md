## 1. NEW MATERIAL — Korean sunscreens sold in US stores are reformulated

**This is the strongest K-beauty claim Seoul Sister has produced. Lead with it.**

Korean-brand sunscreens sold in US retail are **reformulated with FDA-approved
filters**, because Korea approves UV filters the US does not. They are not the
same product as the Korean-market version and they test substantially weaker.

Consumer Reports (July 10 2026) tested each brand's Korean formula against **that
same brand's US formula**:

| Brand | Korean formula | US formula |
|---|---|---|
| Beauty of Joseon | SPF **36** | SPF **19** |
| Innisfree | SPF **48** | SPF **16** |
| Round Lab | SPF **46** | SPF **16** |

Source: `consumerreports.org/health/sunscreens/does-korean-sunscreen-work-better-a2100639078/`

**Why this works as content:** it is counterintuitive, cites a named study with
numbers, and is genuinely useful — someone buying the Target version thinking
they're getting the Korean formula is under-protected. That is the same shape as
the account's best-performing comment (contrarian gap → specific facts →
useful).

**Three calibrations that are NOT optional.** Each one is a thing a knowledgeable
commenter will catch:

- **Lead on PROTECTION, never on texture or elegance.** The same CR sensory panel
  found the Korean sunscreens "required a fair amount of effort to rub in,"
  initially "felt greasy," left "a white cast," and **"none stood out as being
  super lightweight."** Do not write that Korean sunscreens are lightweight or
  cosmetically elegant. That claim is contradicted by the very study you are
  citing.
- **The finding is RELATIVE, not absolute.** Even the Korean formulas came in
  **under** their SPF 50+ labels. The claim is "Korean beats US," not "Korean is
  label-accurate."
- **The filter gap is NARROWING.** The FDA approved **bemotrizinol** in June 2026
  — its first new sunscreen filter since 1999, effective August 2026. Others
  remain unapproved. Do not describe the gap as permanent, and do not describe it
  as closed.

If personal importing comes up: it is a **legal grey area** — an
unapproved-in-the-US formulation where small personal quantities are generally
tolerated but enforcement is inconsistent. State that. Never write "it's illegal"
and never write "it's totally fine."

## 2. STOP — the Amazon commingling claim is stale

**Amazon ended commingled inventory on March 31 2026.** Any copy saying
"commingling means Amazon can ship you a counterfeit" is now factually out of
date and will be corrected in the replies.

Use instead, when counterfeit risk is the topic:
- The risk is **third-party sellers**, and a brand's official storefront is the
  safe path.
- The concrete, documented finding: an independently tested counterfeit
  sunscreen claiming **SPF 45 measured SPF 3.6**, UVB-only, no UVA protection.

**Never claim counterfeits have harmed anyone.** There is **no documented case of
verified medical harm.** Say "failed protection" — it is true, it is specific,
and it makes the point without an unsupportable injury claim.

## 3. NEVER USE — the "KTRI 68%" statistic

Do not publish this claim in any form:

> "A 2022 KTRI study found 68% of COSRX Aloe sunscreen from unauthorized Amazon
> sellers failed SPF testing, and 22% contained methylisothiazolinone."

It traces **only to Alibaba-hosted SEO content.** No primary source, no news
coverage, no brand confirmation. It is the most quotable-sounding and least
supported claim in this space — exactly the kind that gets repeated into
published content and becomes a liability.

If a counterfeit statistic is needed, use COSRX's own public warning about its
own product, or the SPF 3.6 lab finding above.

## 4. NEW POSTURE — Western drugstore brands may be endorsed

Seoul Sister's advisor is now explicitly permitted to tell someone their Western
product is good and to keep it. Generated content should match.

**Genuinely good, do not recommend switching away from:** CeraVe, Cetaphil,
La Roche-Posay, Vanicream, Byoma, Naturium, Prequel. (Cetaphil was reformulated —
older criticism of it is stale.)

Recommend a Korean alternative **only where it genuinely wins for that person**:
a better-suited formula, a meaningfully better price, an active they can't get
otherwise, or the sunscreen filter case above.

**Why this matters for Reddit specifically:** r/AsianBeauty is hostile to
anything reading as a pitch. A commenter who says "your drugstore moisturizer is
fine, don't replace it" earns standing that a recommender never does. "Opinions
about formulas, not about countries" is the line.

Useful for any "I can only buy what's in a store" objection:

Target carries a broad K-beauty range (COSRX, Laneige, medicube, Round Lab,
SKIN1004, Abib, AESTURA, Beauty of Joseon, Goodal, Heimish, ISNtree, MISSHA,

**Never claim a specific product is on a specific store's shelf.** Assortments
vary by location and format; the online range is wider than any one store. "Target
carries COSRX" is safe. "Your Target has the Snail Mucin Essence" is not
knowable.

**Sephora × Olive Young:** a curated Olive Young K-beauty zone launches **autumn
2026**, online and in stores (US, Canada, Hong Kong, Singapore, Malaysia,
Thailand; Middle East / UK / Australia in 2027). Confirmed on Sephora's own
newsroom. **No door count and no brand lineup have been announced** — the widely
repeated "~700 doors" figure is trade press only, so attribute it or omit it, and
do not name brands.
