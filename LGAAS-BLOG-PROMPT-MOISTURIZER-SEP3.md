THE ORGANIZING IDEA: a moisturizer is a delivery system with three jobs, and most
people buy the wrong one because the label does not say which job it does.
  - humectant (draws water in: glycerin, hyaluronic acid, betaine)
  - emollient (softens and smooths: squalane, fatty acids)
  - occlusive (seals it in: petrolatum, shea, heavier silicones)
Teach the reader to read the first five INCI lines and match the formula to their
own skin, instead of ranking brands.

THE MOST USEFUL CORRECTION, give it real space: the hydrator vs moisturizer
distinction. A light water-cream floods water in but leaves almost nothing to
seal it, so on genuinely dry skin it feels good for an hour and then tighter than
before. Water without a lid evaporates and can leave skin drier than it started.
This is the single most common mistake in this category.

ALSO COVER:
- Damaged barrier vs simply dehydrated, and how to tell them apart. Damaged
  stings and reacts to products that used to be fine; dehydrated feels tight and
  looks dull but does not sting. They need different products, and this is the
  exact question the top-ranking long-tail query asks.
- Ceramides as the mortar between skin cells. Dry and damaged skin is short on
  them, so you are replacing what is missing rather than just adding water.
- Skin types: dehydrated-but-oily (gel-cream, humectant-led, light occlusive),
  truly dry (ceramides plus a real occlusive), combination (why one product for
  the whole face is usually the mistake), sensitive or reactive (fragrance-free,
  short ingredient lists), and barrier repair after actives like retinoids or acids.
- Seasonal switching: the same skin often needs a different seal in winter.

- Say plainly that a good Western moisturizer is fine to keep. CeraVe, Vanicream,
