## Why the framing matters more than it should

A stranger scrolling has one question in the first second: *why should I listen to you?*

- **"Since creating the app"** answers: *because I'm promoting my product.* → swipe
- **"My skin was a disaster so I helped build something that could tell me why"** answers:
  *because I had your problem and did something about it.* → keep watching

Same disclosure. Same honesty. One is a reason to leave and one is a reason to stay.

## The three hooks

Each says you made it in the first breath. On-screen text `my app` early, small is fine.
Caption's first line carries it too. All three, every time — then you never think about it.

### 1. The origin — use this one if you pin a video

> **"My skin was a disaster and nobody could tell me why. So I helped build an app that could."**

Explains the entire account in one line, which is what a pinned video is for. It makes your
involvement the *credibility* instead of the disclaimer.

### 2. The reversal — film this one first

> **"I was about to spend $34 on this. I asked my own app, and it told me not to."**

This is the one the data actually supports. The single confirmed cold customer found Seoul
Sister and paid **because Yuri talked her out of two purchases.** "My own app told me not to
buy it" is a stronger trust signal than any endorsement — and the disclosure is what makes it
land. You cannot tell that story without saying you own it.

Pairs with screen-recorded proof. **Stay silent 3-4 seconds and let them read Yuri's answer.**

### 3. The regret — closest to your proven voice

> **"Everything TikTok made me buy that I regret. I built an app so this stops happening to me."**

Your regret/skepticism videos are your real organic performers (486 and 379 views, no
promotion). This keeps that lane and makes the app the *resolution* rather than the subject.
