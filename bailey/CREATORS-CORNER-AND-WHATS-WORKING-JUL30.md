## 2. The thing I'd want you to know even if you'd never asked

I went through your feed. Your hooks are good, your face-to-camera delivery is good, and the Medicube carousel had better judgment in it than most full-time skincare creators manage.

But a large share of your recent posts are follower-goal and follow-back content — *"Current: 448, Goal: 1000," "I'll be here following all my girls back," "let's get each other there in the comments," "F4F with the girls."*

**In 2026 that's not neutral. It works against you.**

Here's the mechanism, and it changed recently. TikTok now **tests a new video against your existing followers first.** How *they* respond decides whether it goes wider. So your followers aren't a trophy anymore — they're the **jury** for every video you post.

Follow-for-follow recruits people who followed you to be followed back. They don't care about skincare. They don't watch to the end. And now they're the first audience every one of your videos gets graded by.

**You didn't buy followers. You bought jurors who don't like skincare.** That's why the number climbing hasn't turned into reach.

There's a second cost: TikTok also looks at engagement *relative to* follower count. Growing followers faster than engagement makes the ratio look worse, not better. F4F pushes both dials the wrong way at once.

**What to do:** just stop. No purge, no drama, no announcement — dead followers dilute slowly and get outvoted as real ones arrive. Stop making follower-goal content and stop the follow-back trades. Your view counts are already fairly even (219–1,025), which means FYP isn't pushing you much yet, and this is a large part of why.

*One honest caveat: this is mechanism-level evidence reported consistently across sources, not a controlled experiment — nobody's run one. But it points one direction, and F4F has no upside to weigh against it.*

## 4. What the format evidence actually says

The clearest finding across everything I read: **in skincare, ingredient-explainer content answering a real comment has the best performance of any format.** Better than GRWM, better than hauls, better than shelfies.

Not a coincidence — it's the collision of three things. TikTok search is now a primary discovery surface (about half of Gen Z starts product research in TikTok search, not Google). Ingredient questions are exactly what people type. And in an AI-slop-saturated feed, a real person answering a real question is the thing AI can't fake — brands are now paying *premiums* for visible human "messiness."

Practical version:

- **Say the search phrase out loud in the first two seconds**, put it on screen, put it in the caption. "Korean sunscreen at Sephora" — say it, don't imply it.
- **Reply-to-comment videos.** Every real question in your comments is a video. This is the top-performing format and it costs you nothing to produce.
- **Keep the topic fixed, vary the format.** Posting across unrelated topics is what hurts — not personality. Your life content is fine *when it comes through the skincare lens*. Your ATX life, your routine, your regrets: that's lifestyle as a skincare vehicle, and it works.
- **Sub-60 seconds** while you're growing; 21–34s has the best completion. (Longer 1–3 min video only matters once you're chasing Creator Rewards.)
- **3–5 genuinely good posts a week.** Not 10, not 30. Volume only helps above a quality floor, and weak videos now drag your account's future reach down with them.
- **Lead with why you're worth listening to.** You're a nurse, you've used this app since February, you argued the AI into changing the product. Credentials belong in the first two seconds, not the bio.
