# 1. Your three questions

## Will being open about Seoul Sister hurt your brand?

**No. It is the strongest thing you have.**

Audiences in 2026 judge the incentive before they judge the recommendation. That sounds like bad news until you compare your two income sources side by side.

With TikTok Shop, you get paid when someone buys the thing you are holding. Your interest and the viewer's are not aligned.

With Seoul Sister, you get paid when someone's skin actually gets better and they keep subscribing. Your interest and the viewer's are identical.

The second one is rare, and you can say it out loud: *"I make money if this actually works for you. That is exactly why I built it."*

The real risk is not owning it. The risk is being boring or salesy about it.

## Should you soft-pedal it and just mention it casually?

**No, and this one has legal teeth.**

The FTC treats a material connection as anything that could sway a recommendation, and says explicitly that it does not matter how small the benefit is. Owning part of the product is the biggest material connection there is. Disclosure has to be clear and unmissable, in the video and above the "more" cut in the caption. Hashtags alone are specifically called out as not enough.

Strategically it is also the worst of both worlds. You would pay the full credibility cost of promoting something and get none of the founder-story upside. And it is fragile. Your bio already links seoulsister.com/tt. Someone will connect it eventually, and being asked is much worse than having told.

## How should you describe your role?

Avoid leading with a title. Say the specific thing instead:

> *"I built this with my dad."*
>
> *"I use this every day and I am the one who tells them what is broken."*

Specific beats a title. It is more credible, more interesting, and it holds up if anyone pushes back. Your very first video already had the right voice, the "me one random Sunday deciding to post" one. Trust that instinct.

# 2. The two hard rules

## Rule 1: Never mix a Shop tag with a Seoul Sister mention

You are a TikTok Shop affiliate now, so this matters more than anything else in this document.

TikTok Shop penalizes sending traffic off app inside Shop-tagged content, and it catches implied references, not just explicit ones. The reported penalty is 2 to 3 points per violation, and **24 points in 90 days gets you banned.** An AI does the reviewing, not a person, so you do not get the benefit of the doubt.

**Shop-tagged video:** do not mention Seoul Sister. No "link in bio," no "check my profile," no hinting. Nothing.

**Seoul Sister video:** no Shop tag on it. Talk freely.

**Your bio link is completely fine.** A link in your bio is normal creator behavior and is not a violation. The rule is about pointing people off-platform from inside a Shop-tagged video. Static bio link, no verbal pointer from Shop content, and you are clean.

Protect the account. It is your distribution.

## Rule 2: Tell your story, never write a symptom checklist

Your face, your story, other people's stories are all encouraged. That is the whole channel.

The one narrow thing to avoid is turning a story into a diagnosis.

**Do not say:** "Here is how you know if YOUR barrier is damaged: stinging, redness, tightness." That is a symptom checklist, and someone with a completely different condition will self-diagnose off it.

**Do say:** "I wrecked my skin last year. For me it was stinging and that tight feeling, and it took me way too long to realize I was over-exfoliating."

Same information. Same story. No diagnosis. And honestly it is the better video, because it sounds like a person instead of a medical website.

Same move for counterfeits. Teach what the markers mean. Do not frame it as protecting people from harm.

# 3. How often to post

**About 10 videos a week, not 21.**

I know that is the opposite of the three-a-day plan. Here is why.

The algorithm now weights watch time and completion rate over views. Quality beats volume, explicitly. Niche consistency is heavily rewarded, and creators posting across three or more unrelated topics see roughly **45% lower reach.** Your three planned pillars, Seoul Sister plus daily life plus Shop products, read as three different topics. That is the exact pattern being penalized.

Three to five posts a week is the commonly cited 2026 sweet spot. Three focused videos often beat seven scattered ones. And consistency beats bursts. Seven posts in one day then a week off is worse than one a day for a week.

## The weekly mix

| Format | Per week | Shop tag? |
|---|---|---|
| Skincare value (Seoul Sister often invisible) | ~4 | No |
| Product content (TikTok Shop) | ~3 | **Yes, zero SS mention** |
| Founder / behind the scenes | ~2 | No |
| Life / personality | ~1 | No |

Think of it as **one niche with four formats**, not three pillars. Your niche is already right and already in your bio: skincare and beauty. Daily life should be seasoning inside the other formats, not its own lane. That is what authenticity means to the algorithm: personality in the delivery, consistency in the topic.

# 4. The script formula

**Every Seoul Sister video: 20 to 35 seconds.**

| Beat | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook, hands moving | 0 to 3s | Confession or a flipped assumption. Motion holds the eye while the claim lands. |
| 2. **The demo** | 3 to 20s | **Screen-record the actual thing happening.** This is what converts. Do not talk over it. |
| 3. The honest beat | 20 to 30s | If you are telling people not to buy something, offer no alternative product. |
| 4. Name it once | ~30s | "It is called Seoul Sister." Nothing more. |

## Why the demo matters more than anything else

The best evidence we have is Cal AI, an app at almost exactly Seoul Sister's price. It reached roughly $2M a month almost entirely through TikTok, using about 1,000 short videos that **show the product doing something impressive and then name it.** People subscribed after a single video, having never seen the creator before.

The thing that converted was the demo, not the pitch and not weeks of building trust.

So: show Yuri actually doing the thing. Catching a counterfeit. Tearing apart an ingredient list. Talking you out of a purchase. Talking *about* an AI advisor is abstract. Watching one work is not.

## Some rules for every video

- Hook under 12 words, hands doing something
- On-screen text from the first frame, most people watch muted
- One idea per video
- Deliver the full value, then name the product. Never tease and gate.
- Say the brand name out loud rather than relying on "link in bio." Not because "link in bio" is penalized, that turns out to be a myth, but because a spoken name survives duets, reposts and screen recordings where the bio disappears
- No dashes in captions. They read as AI-written.

## What not to do

Do not put the price in the hook. Do not say "sign up now." Do not ask for follows, likes or shares, that is real engagement bait and it is penalized. Do not run a CTA with no demo behind it, there is nothing to want. And do not tease and gate, as in "the full routine is in my bio," because withholding value to force a click is the actual bait pattern.

# 8. Shop scripts

**Zero Seoul Sister mention on any of these. No off-platform pointer. Not even implied.**

Your Shop videos work completely differently from your Seoul Sister videos, and you should stop thinking of them as the same job. Seoul Sister is a considered subscription, so those videos demo a thing and let it speak. Shop is impulse purchase, see it and want it and buy it in ninety seconds, and there is a proven template for exactly that.

## The 7-beat Shop template

Your dad sent me a video from a creator doing around $20k a month on TikTok Shop who teaches her exact format. I looked at it closely. **For your Shop videos, it is very good, and I would run almost all of it.**

| # | Beat | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook, verbal and visual** | Say something slightly surprising *while your hands are doing something*. Applying, swatching, removing. Motion holds the eye while the claim lands. |
| 2 | **Social proof** | "This is the one everyone is talking about." Borrows credibility instantly. |
| 3 | **Sell the dream** | Name the problems people have with this product category, then why this one solves them. |
| 4 | **Handle both objections** | The product objection ("is it sticky?") AND the decision objection ("which shade do I even pick?"). |
| 5 | **Price and deal** | If it is genuinely on sale or a bundle, say so. |
| 6 | **Social proof again, personal** | "My friends keep asking me about this." |
| 7 | **Casual close** | Low key. Not a hard sell. |

**Beat 4 is the one most creators miss.** There are two different reasons someone does not buy. One is a problem with the product. The other is that they cannot decide which version is right for them, so they close the app and never come back. That second one is invisible and it kills more sales than the first. Answer it out loud: "the honey mango shade works on basically any skin tone" removes the paralysis.

**One change I would make to her template.** She says her goal with the closing line is to make it "as organic as possible so people do not know I am trying to sell them." Skip that part. You are an affiliate, so you need the Branded Content toggle on and clear disclosure either way, and hiding it is both a rule problem and unnecessary. Keep her casual tone, drop the concealment. "It is linked below if you want it" is casual and honest at the same time. You lose nothing.

**And keep beat 5 honest.** TikTok Shop runs real sales constantly, so mentioning one is just true. Do not invent urgency that is not there. Reporting a real discount is fine. Manufacturing a fake one is the thing that burns trust.

# 9. What to expect, honestly

Your follower count barely affects your reach. TikTok decides per video, not per account, which is how you went from zero to a thousand in a week. So 1,182 followers is not the thing holding you back.

But the math from video views to actual signups is brutal, and you should know it going in:

> A 10,000 view video gets roughly 30 to 150 people to the site, which is maybe 0 to 2 signups.
>
> An ordinary 500 view video gets approximately zero.

**Signups will arrive in lumps from the occasional video that takes off, not as a steady trickle.** Cal AI's advantage was volume: they posted constantly so the occasional hit had chances to happen.

**For the next 60 days, judge this on how many people come to the site and start a conversation with Yuri, not on paid subscribers.** One paid subscriber from TikTok would be a genuine win. Several weeks of zero is normal and is not a sign it is failing.

We can see exactly how many people arrive from your link. That number is currently zero, so the first one will be unmistakable.

Ten people who actually talk to Yuri beats a 100,000 view video that sends nobody.

# 10. The experiment

There is a real disagreement about whether these videos should ask people to visit the site at all. One view says never pitch, let the bio do the work. The other says a video with no ask produces nothing.

Nobody has actually tested this properly. There is no study either way. So rather than guess, your first twelve Seoul Sister videos will settle it.

| Group | What you do | Which scripts |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Say the name out loud at the end | R-1, R-2, R-3, R-7 |
| **B** | Say nothing, put the link in a pinned comment | R-4, R-6, R-8, R-10 |
| **C** | No mention at all | R-5, R-9 |

Each script above already tells you which group it belongs to, so you do not have to think about it. Just film them and post.

After about four to six weeks we compare how many site visits each group produced per thousand views, and whichever wins becomes the rule.

**My prediction, written down now so it can be proven wrong:** group A wins and group C produces almost nothing. If I am wrong, we will know, and we will do what the data says instead.
